The Basics

Organic chemistry names follow IUPAC (International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry) rules. Most names are built from up to four parts:

  1. Locants / substituent prefixes — numbers and names of branches or attached groups (e.g. 2-methyl-, 3-chloro-).
  2. Root prefix — how many carbon atoms are in the main chain (e.g. meth-, eth-, prop-).
  3. Infix — position of unsaturation or ring (e.g. but-2-en-).
  4. Suffix — the principal functional group (e.g. -ane, -ol, -oic acid).

Example: 3-methylbut-1-ene = 3-methyl (branch on carbon 3) + but (4 carbons) + 1-ene (double bond starting at carbon 1).
